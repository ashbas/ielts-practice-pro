IELTS READING GUIDE

Read for structure, then search for evidence

Learn how to approach passages and question types without getting trapped by individual keywords.

What makes IELTS Reading difficult?

Many candidates know the vocabulary in a passage but lose time because they read everything at the same speed. A better approach is to understand the overall structure first and then move to the part of the passage that can answer a particular question.

Skimming

Skimming means reading quickly to understand the purpose and organization of a text. Notice headings, topic sentences, repeated concepts and transitions. You are not trying to remember every detail.

Scanning

Scanning is targeted searching. Use distinctive words from the question, but remember that the passage may use a synonym or paraphrase. Once you locate a likely section, slow down and read enough context to confirm the meaning.

Question types

True/False/Not Given: compare the statement with the passage. “Not Given” means the passage does not provide enough information to establish the statement.

Matching headings: focus on the main idea of the paragraph, not one attractive keyword.

Multiple choice: eliminate options that contradict the passage or answer a different question.

Sentence and summary completion: predict the type of word needed before searching, then check grammar and meaning.

Matching information: search for distinctive facts, names, dates or ideas, while allowing for paraphrase.

Timing strategy

Use a time plan that leaves enough room to review answers. If one question is consuming several minutes, make the best decision you can, mark it mentally, and move forward. Spending too long on one item can cause several later questions to be rushed.

Evidence beats intuition

If an answer feels correct but you cannot point to the relevant part of the passage, keep looking. IELTS Reading questions are designed around information in the text. Your background knowledge is not a substitute for textual evidence.

Practice routine

  1. Complete a short passage without a timer.
  2. Review every wrong answer and identify why it was wrong.
  3. Repeat a similar question type under time pressure.
  4. Keep a list of recurring errors, such as confusing Not Given with False.
