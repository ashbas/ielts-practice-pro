IELTS VOCABULARY

Learn words you can actually use

Build vocabulary through meaning, collocations and examples instead of memorizing isolated synonym lists.

Accuracy comes before complexity

IELTS preparation is not a competition to use the rarest word you know. A familiar word used accurately is more useful than an advanced word used in the wrong context. Learn each new word with its common partners and grammatical pattern.

Education vocabulary

access to education — Many students still have limited access to quality education.

academic performance — Sleep and concentration can affect academic performance.

vocational training — Vocational training can prepare learners for practical occupations.

lifelong learning — Rapid changes in technology make lifelong learning increasingly useful.

Environment vocabulary

carbon emissions — Public transport can help reduce carbon emissions.

renewable energy — Investment in renewable energy can reduce dependence on fossil fuels.

natural habitat — Construction can place pressure on natural habitats.

environmental impact — Consumers are increasingly aware of the environmental impact of products.

Work and society vocabulary

work-life balance — Flexible schedules may improve work-life balance for some employees.

job satisfaction — Salary is only one factor that can influence job satisfaction.

social inequality — Education and employment opportunities can affect social inequality.

public services — Reliable public services can improve quality of life.

Useful verbs

address a problem, contribute to a change, enhance quality, reduce risk, promote access, tackle inequality, allocate resources, and implement a policy.

How to learn vocabulary

  1. Learn the word in a sentence.
  2. Record one common collocation.
  3. Write your own example.
  4. Say the sentence aloud.
  5. Use the word again a few days later.

Avoid synonym swapping

Words that look similar are not always interchangeable. “Economic” and “economical,” for example, have different meanings. Always check the sentence pattern and context before replacing a familiar word.
