IELTS WRITING GUIDE

Plan clearly. Write directly.

Understand the difference between Writing Task 1 and Task 2 and build a repeatable approach to planning and drafting.

Task 1 and Task 2 are different jobs

For Academic Writing, Task 1 asks you to summarize or report information such as a graph, table, chart, process or map. Task 2 asks you to write an essay responding to a question or issue. For General Training, Task 1 is a letter. In both versions, planning and answering the exact task are more important than inserting impressive vocabulary.

A reliable planning routine

Spend a few minutes identifying the task, deciding what information is most important, and choosing a paragraph structure. For Task 2, write your position in one sentence before you start. For Task 1, identify the main features or overall trend before describing individual details.

Paragraph structure

A useful body paragraph normally has a controlling idea, explanation and evidence or example. The exact structure can change with the question. The goal is a logical progression rather than a rigid formula.

Coherence and cohesion

Readers should be able to understand how your sentences relate to each other. Use pronouns, repeated key terms, logical paragraphing and linking expressions when they genuinely help. Avoid putting a connector such as “Moreover” at the beginning of every sentence simply because you learned it as a useful phrase.

Vocabulary

Prefer accurate words over unusual ones. If you can explain an idea naturally using familiar academic vocabulary, that is usually safer than using a word you only know from a memorized list. Pay attention to collocations: words that naturally occur together are often more useful than isolated synonyms.

Grammar

Work on accuracy in the structures you actually use. Check subject–verb agreement, articles, verb tense, sentence boundaries, countable nouns and punctuation. A small number of reliable complex structures is more useful than sentences that become difficult to understand.

Common reasons practice essays become weak

  • The response does not fully answer every part of the question.
  • The introduction promises one position while the body argues another.
  • Examples are mentioned but not explained.
  • Paragraphs contain several unrelated ideas.
  • Vocabulary is forced or used incorrectly.
  • The writer spends too much time on the introduction and rushes the conclusion.

Final five-minute check

Check whether every paragraph supports the task. Then scan for repeated words, obvious grammar errors, missing articles, spelling mistakes and incomplete sentences. Do not rewrite entire paragraphs during the final minutes unless a major problem requires it.
