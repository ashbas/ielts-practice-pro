SPEAKING PART 2

How to keep a cue-card answer going

Learn a practical four-part structure for your one-minute preparation and two-minute response.

Use the preparation minute well

Use the minute to create a small map of your answer, not a written speech. Note a place, person, event or object and then add three or four supporting ideas. Short words are faster to write and easier to glance at while speaking.

A flexible four-part structure

  1. Introduce it: say what or who you are describing.
  2. Give context: explain where, when or how you encountered it.
  3. Develop the main details: explain two or three points from the cue card.
  4. Reflect: say why it mattered, how you felt, or what you would do next.

10 original practice cue cards

  1. Describe a place where you feel relaxed.
  2. Describe a useful item you bought recently.
  3. Describe a person who taught you an important skill.
  4. Describe a journey you remember clearly.
  5. Describe a meal you enjoyed with other people.
  6. Describe a skill you would like to learn.
  7. Describe a time you solved a difficult problem.
  8. Describe a building you find interesting.
  9. Describe a piece of advice that helped you.
  10. Describe an activity you would recommend to a visitor.

How to avoid running out of things to say

If your answer is becoming short, move from description to explanation. Instead of listing more facts, explain why something mattered, compare it with another experience, describe what happened next, or imagine how the situation could be different in the future.

Do not memorize a model answer

Model answers are useful for noticing organization and vocabulary, but memorizing one can make your response sound unnatural and may not fit the exact prompt. Practice the same cue card twice using different details.

Two-minute practice routine

Set a one-minute preparation timer. Write four keywords. Then speak for two minutes without stopping the recording. Listen back and mark three places where you could have developed an idea further. Repeat the task later.
