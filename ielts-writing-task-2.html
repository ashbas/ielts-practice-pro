WRITING TASK 2

A practical approach to IELTS essays

Identify the question type, choose a position, develop relevant ideas and keep your essay focused.

Step 1: identify the task

Read the final sentence carefully. “To what extent do you agree or disagree?” requires a position. “Discuss both views and give your own opinion” requires discussion of both perspectives plus your view. “What are the causes and what solutions can you suggest?” requires causes and solutions.

Step 2: decide your position

Write a one-sentence answer before drafting. You do not need an extreme position if the question allows a balanced response. What matters is that the position is clear and remains consistent.

Step 3: choose two strong ideas

Two developed ideas are often more useful than four undeveloped ones. For each idea, ask: Why is this true? What example could demonstrate it? What consequence follows? If you cannot answer these questions, choose a different idea.

A four-paragraph option

Introduction: paraphrase the issue and state your position. Body 1: develop your first main idea. Body 2: develop your second main idea or address another required part of the question. Conclusion: restate the central position without introducing a new argument.

15 original practice questions

  1. Some people believe schools should teach practical financial skills. To what extent do you agree?
  2. Many people now work remotely. Discuss the advantages and disadvantages.
  3. Should cities spend more money on public transport than on roads? Discuss both views.
  4. Some believe children should spend more time outdoors. What are the benefits and how can this be encouraged?
  5. Technology makes communication easier but can reduce face-to-face interaction. Discuss both views.
  6. Should employers allow a four-day working week? Give reasons for your answer.
  7. Some people think university education should be free for everyone. To what extent do you agree?
  8. What are the main causes of food waste, and what measures could reduce it?
  9. Advertising encourages people to buy things they do not need. Do you agree or disagree?
  10. Should governments invest more in preventing illness than treating it?
  11. Tourism can bring economic benefits but also create problems for local communities. Discuss.
  12. Some people prefer living in large cities while others prefer smaller communities. Discuss both views.
  13. Is it better for young people to choose a career based on income or personal interest?
  14. Many traditional skills are disappearing. Why is this happening and is it a problem?
  15. Should students be allowed to use artificial intelligence tools for school assignments? Discuss the benefits and risks.

Example planning notes

Question: “Should cities spend more money on public transport than roads?” Position: prioritize public transport in dense urban areas. Idea 1: capacity and congestion. Example: frequent buses or trains move many people using limited road space. Idea 2: access and environmental impact. Example: reliable public transport can help people who do not own cars and may reduce individual car journeys.

Final reminder

Do not write what you think an examiner wants to hear. Write a clear answer to the actual question and support it logically.
