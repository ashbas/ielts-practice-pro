SPEAKING PART 3

Explain your ideas, not just your opinion

Part 3 questions invite broader discussion. Practice moving from a clear opinion to a reason, example and implication.

A four-step answer

Start with your position. Then explain why you think that way. Add a concrete example or comparison. Finally, explain the consequence or qualify your answer if appropriate.

Example: “I think public transport should be improved before cities build more roads. The reason is that a reliable train or bus system can move many people without requiring every person to drive. For example, commuters may choose public transport when services are frequent and predictable. That can also reduce pressure on busy roads.”

Technology

  1. How has technology changed the way people learn?
  2. Do people rely too heavily on smartphones?
  3. Should children have limits on their use of technology?
  4. How might artificial intelligence change workplaces?

Travel and communities

  1. Why do people want to travel to unfamiliar places?
  2. How can tourism affect local residents?
  3. Should popular destinations limit the number of visitors?
  4. What makes a city attractive to tourists?

Education

  1. Should schools focus more on practical skills?
  2. What makes a teacher effective?
  3. How might education change over the next twenty years?
  4. Is competition between students helpful?

How to sound natural

You can qualify an answer. Phrases such as “It depends on the situation,” “In many cases,” and “I would say that…” allow you to make a nuanced point without pretending every situation is identical. Then explain the condition that changes your answer.

Practice method

Choose one question and record a 45–60 second answer. Listen back and underline the sentence containing your main opinion. Then mark whether you gave a reason and a specific example. Repeat the question with a different example.
