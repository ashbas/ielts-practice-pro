READING QUESTION TYPES

Know what each question is asking you to prove

A question-type strategy helps you search the passage efficiently while still checking the surrounding context.

True / False / Not Given

Separate the three ideas carefully. True means the statement agrees with information in the passage. False means the passage contradicts it. Not Given means the passage does not provide enough information to decide. Do not choose False merely because you cannot find the statement.

Matching headings

Read the paragraph as a unit. Ask what the paragraph is mainly doing: introducing a problem, explaining a cause, describing a change, presenting research, or giving an example. A heading that matches one sentence but not the paragraph's central idea is usually a distractor.

Multiple choice

Read the question first and identify its focus. Then compare each option with the passage. Be cautious when an option repeats words from the text but changes the meaning.

Sentence completion

Look at the grammar around the blank. Predict whether you need a noun, verb, number or phrase. Then locate the relevant section and copy only the amount of information allowed by the instructions.

Summary completion

A summary usually follows the order of ideas in a particular part of the passage. Read the completed sentence mentally and check whether the answer fits both grammar and meaning.

Matching information

Look for distinctive information such as a person's name, a study, an unusual example or a specific claim. Once found, read the entire surrounding paragraph before deciding.

Common trap: keyword matching

A question may use a word that also appears in the passage, but the answer may depend on a paraphrase several lines away. Train yourself to match meaning rather than identical vocabulary.

Mini practice

Passage statement: “The research team originally expected the change to occur gradually, but the data showed a rapid shift.” Question: “The researchers predicted that the change would happen quickly.” The correct classification would be False, because the passage says their original expectation was gradual.
