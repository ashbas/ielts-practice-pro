PASSAGE

Why urban trees matter

Cities around the world are planting more trees as planners look for practical ways to improve urban environments. Trees can provide shade, reduce the temperature of surfaces such as pavements, and make some public spaces more comfortable during hot weather.

The benefits are not limited to temperature. Urban vegetation can also provide habitats for birds and insects, although the value of a particular tree or planting scheme depends on the species used and the surrounding environment. Some cities have therefore begun selecting plants that are better adapted to local conditions.

Trees can also influence how people experience a neighbourhood. Research in several cities has found associations between access to green spaces and people's perceptions of their local environment. However, researchers caution that the presence of trees alone does not guarantee that residents will use a space. Safety, accessibility and the design of the surrounding area can also affect how public spaces are used.

Maintaining urban trees requires planning. Young trees need water while they become established, and mature trees may require pruning or other care. In some locations, roots can also interfere with pavements or underground infrastructure. For this reason, successful urban planting generally involves choosing appropriate species and considering the conditions of the site before trees are planted.

QUESTIONS

True, False or Not Given?

Choose one answer for each statement. Try the questions before revealing the answers.

Answers & explanations

  1. True. The passage says trees can provide shade and make some public spaces more comfortable during hot weather.
  2. False. The value of a tree or planting scheme depends on the species and surrounding environment.
  3. True. Safety, accessibility and surrounding design can affect whether people use a space.
  4. Not Given. The passage says young and mature trees may require different types of care, but does not compare which requires more overall maintenance.
  5. True. Roots can interfere with pavements or underground infrastructure.
  6. Not Given. The passage mentions that some cities have begun selecting plants suited to local conditions, but does not say every major city does this.

READING STRATEGY

Not mentioned means Not Given.

Don't use your general knowledge. If the passage neither confirms nor contradicts a statement, the correct answer is Not Given.
