LISTENING QUESTION TYPES

Use the question format as a listening clue

Different question types require different preparation. Learn what to predict before the recording begins.

Form completion

Forms often ask for practical details such as names, addresses, dates, times or prices. Read the surrounding words and predict the answer type. Pay particular attention to spelling and singular/plural forms.

Note and table completion

Look at the headings and the information around each blank. A table may organize a discussion by category, while notes may follow the speaker's sequence. Use the structure to predict what comes next.

Map and plan labelling

Before listening, identify the starting point, compass directions if provided, and fixed landmarks. Listen for relationships such as next to, opposite, behind, at the end of the corridor and on the left.

Multiple choice

Read all relevant options before the recording. Some options may be mentioned but rejected by the speaker. Listen for the idea that answers the question, not simply a word that appears in one option.

Matching

Matching questions can require you to connect people, places, opinions or activities with a list of choices. Mark the options as you hear them and be prepared for the speaker to discuss more than one option.

Distractors appear in many formats

A speaker can correct a number, change a plan, reject a suggestion or mention an option only to explain why it is unsuitable. The important information is determined by the complete meaning of the sentence.

After each practice test

Do not only record your score. Categorize every mistake: missed because you did not hear it, heard it but spelled it incorrectly, chose the first detail before a correction, lost your place, or misunderstood the question. Different mistakes need different practice.
