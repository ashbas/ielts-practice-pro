IELTS LISTENING GUIDE

Prepare before the recording starts

Use the questions to predict what you will hear, then stay flexible when speakers change or correct information.

Why prediction matters

Before a recording begins, look at the questions and identify the information each blank needs. A blank after “at” may need a time; a blank after “cost” may need a price. Prediction gives your attention a target.

Listen for meaning, not just words

Speakers may paraphrase the wording in the question. If the question asks why a person chose a course, the recording may never use the word “reason.” It may instead say “I decided on it because…”

Watch for distractors

A common listening pattern is correction: a speaker mentions one detail and then changes it. For example, “The meeting is on Tuesday—actually, I mean Wednesday.” The final information is the relevant detail.

Spelling and numbers

Practice names, addresses, dates, prices, times and room numbers separately. When a spelling is dictated, listen for the individual letters rather than assuming how the name is written.

What if you miss an answer?

Keep going. The next question will arrive whether or not you are ready. Mark the missed item and immediately return your attention to the recording. You can make a better guess later than you can recover several questions you missed while worrying about one.

Note completion

Read the surrounding sentence and predict the grammatical form. After writing an answer, check whether it fits the sentence. Also follow the word-limit instruction exactly.

Practice routine

  1. Do a short exercise without pausing.
  2. Check answers and read the transcript.
  3. Listen again to the sentences containing mistakes.
  4. Write down why you missed each answer.
  5. Repeat a similar exercise a few days later.
